Everything you copy, put to work

Your Mac forgets a copy the moment you make the next one.

MemoryClip keeps it, reads it, and turns the ones worth keeping into notes and calendar events.

Find anything you copied

Private by construction

A searchable history

Press ⇧⌘V anywhere and every recent copy is there — text, links, images, files, colours. Type a sentence to find one, Return to paste it. A key:value operator narrows to when (after:7d) or where (app:Safari) it came from.

Turns a screenshot into a note

One key sends it to Obsidian, a folder of Markdown files, Apple Notes or a Shortcut — with the picture and the text it found, filed by the day you captured it.

Pinboards for the keepers

Named sets — addresses, canned replies, commands — as chips over the clips. ⌘P files one under a board, and inside a board clips keep the order you put them in.

Keeps your keys encrypted

Copy an API key, a token or a one-time code and it is sealed with a key that lives in this Mac's Secure Enclave. The history shows a mask; the value asks for Touch ID before it is shown or pasted.

It reads your screenshots

Take a screenshot and MemoryClip pulls the text out of it, in 30 languages, so you can search for a picture by what it said.

Translates as you look

Copy something in a language you don't read — or screenshot it — and the preview shows it in one you do, a ⌘T away from the original.

Nothing leaves your Mac

No sync, no accounts, no analytics. Everything runs on your machine, including the language models. The one network call it can make is an update check you switch on yourself.

Cleans the links you copy

Tracking parameters — fbclid, gclid and friends — are stripped on the way in, and the cleaned link goes straight back on the clipboard. Your next ⌘V pastes it without opening the panel.

Offers a date as an event

Copy an invitation, or screenshot one, and ⌘E makes it an event — the time, the Zoom link, the address. With suggestions on, an appointment-shaped clip is offered: Add, Edit…, or Not Now, and nothing is written until you say so.

Open while you work

The panel opens over whatever you're doing and closes the moment you're done. Newest first, already focused. ⌘S saves one as a note.

Search

Search in your own words.

Say roughly what it said, or say where and when it came from. Both work as you type.

Operators — narrow to where and when

app:clips from an app, matched by name prefix — app:saf reaches Safari
is:a state — pinned, screenshot, noted, event, translated, secret
in:a pinboard, matched by name prefix (in:pinned is Pinned)
type:a clip kind — text, image, link, file, color
after:copied on or after — today, yesterday, 2026-09-01, 7d, 2w, 3m
before:copied before — the same values
on:copied on one day — the same values

Sentences — say what it was about

that error about the deploy failing→ finds the clip that says deployment to production failed
failing→ reaches failed. screenshots reaches screenshot. Words are reduced to the stem they share with their dictionary form, and a stem is only ever a prefix of what you typed — so it can widen a search and never narrow it.
app:"Google Chrome" -app:Slack→ a value with spaces takes quotes, and a leading - excludes.

Typing a key and : offers the values it knows — source apps most-used first, pinboards in strip order. A lone ? in the field shows the whole table as a cheat-sheet.

Install

Installing it

Five minutes, and one screen that will try to stop you. Here's how to get past it.

  1. Download the app

    Use the Download for Mac button — it gives you a file ending in .dmg, in your Downloads folder.

  2. Drag it into Applications

    Double-click the downloaded file. A window opens with the MemoryClip icon and an Applications folder. Drag the icon onto the folder, then close the window.

  3. Try to open it — and let macOS refuse

    Open your Applications folder and double-click MemoryClip. macOS will say it cannot be opened because it is not from an identified developer. This is expected. Click Done.

  4. Tell macOS you meant it

    Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to the Security section, and you'll see a line about MemoryClip being blocked. Click Open Anyway, then confirm with Touch ID or your password.

  5. That's it — press ⇧⌘V

    Copy a few things, then press ⇧⌘V from any app. Everything you copied is there, waiting.

Why does macOS block it? Apple charges developers $99 a year to sign apps in a way that opens without a warning. MemoryClip is free and unsigned, so macOS treats it as unknown rather than unsafe — it has no idea what the app is, and says so. Every line of its source is public on GitHub if you or someone you trust would like to look before installing.
Comfortable with Terminal? Use Homebrew instead

Two commands, and brew upgrade keeps it current afterwards. The trust line is Homebrew's own check on third-party sources.

brew tap yaminerl/memoryclip https://github.com/YamineRL/MemoryClip
brew trust yaminerl/memoryclip && brew install --cask memoryclip

This handles the Gatekeeper step for you, so steps 3 and 4 don't apply.

Removing it

Drag MemoryClip from your Applications folder to the Trash — or brew uninstall --cask memoryclip if you installed it that way. Your clip history lives in your user Library folder and goes with the app if you add --zap to the Homebrew command.

Privacy

What it does with your data

Short version: nothing you didn't ask for. The full privacy page →

It never phones home

No sync service, no accounts, no analytics, no crash reporting — the text recognition and translation run on your Mac. It makes exactly one kind of network request, and only if you ask: an anonymous daily read of the public releases page, off by default, which tells you a new version exists and never installs anything itself.

It skips your passwords

Copies marked confidential by password managers are ignored, and card numbers are filtered out of what it reads. An API key or a one-time code is recognised and kept encrypted — sealed with a key in the Secure Enclave that never leaves this Mac — and asking for one back needs Touch ID. You can lock the whole history behind Touch ID, too.

It asks for almost nothing

No Screen Recording, no Input Monitoring. Folder access only for the screenshot folder and the notes folder you choose, and only if you use those features. Calendar access is write-only: it can add an event and cannot read one.

It forgets on a schedule

The newest 5,000 clips are kept and anything older than 90 days is swept away. Both numbers are yours to change, up to Unlimited and a year, and Clear All History is one menu item. Pinned clips are exempt from the sweep.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Why does macOS say it can't be opened?

Apple charges developers $99 a year to sign apps in a way that opens without a warning. MemoryClip is free and unsigned, so macOS treats it as unknown rather than unsafe — it has no idea what the app is, and says so. Steps 3 and 4 of the install get you past it once; Homebrew handles it for you. Every line of the source is public on GitHub if you'd like to look first.

Does it see my passwords?

No. Copies marked confidential by password managers are ignored — the standard pasteboard opt-out markers, and known credential apps outright — and card numbers are filtered out of what it reads. Any app you name in Settings → Privacy → Excluded apps is skipped too. An API key or a one-time code is recognised and kept encrypted with a key in the Secure Enclave, and asking for one back needs Touch ID.

Where does my history live, and for how long?

On this Mac only, in an owner-only store at ~/Library/Application Support/app.memoryclip/. The newest 5,000 clips are kept and anything older than 90 days is swept away; both numbers are yours to change, up to Unlimited and a year. Pinned clips are exempt, and Clear All History is one menu item.

What does the update check send?

Nothing about you. It is off by default, and with it off MemoryClip makes no network calls at all. Switched on in Settings → General → Updates, it asks api.github.com once a day what the newest release is — an unauthenticated request to a public endpoint carrying no identifier of any kind. If there's something newer you get one notification, once per version. It never installs anything itself.

How do I uninstall it?

Drag MemoryClip from your Applications folder to the Trash — or brew uninstall --cask memoryclip if you installed it that way. Your clip history lives in your user Library folder and goes with the app if you add --zap to the Homebrew command.
